Quick reference: timing

Everything is measured in one "dot" unit. Tap a row to hear it:

dot = 1
dash = 3 (three dots long)
gap inside a letter = 1
gap between letters = 3
gap between words = 7

Words to know

  • Dot (say dit): the short signal.
  • Dash (say dah): the long signal, 3 times as long.
  • Morse code: a way to write letters with dots and dashes, invented in the 1830s–40s for the telegraph.
  • Decode: to turn dots and dashes back into letters.

Dots and dashes of prayer

Morse has only two signals, short and long, with silence in between. Prayer is like that: short prayers ("Jesus, I trust in You") and long ones (the Rosary, Adoration), and holy silence between them. God can receive a dot or a dash, and He is listening for you.

Cool facts

  • The most common letters are at the top, so they are the shortest: E is one dot, T is one dash.
  • Level 1 has 2 letters, level 2 has 4, level 3 has 8, level 4 has 12. That makes 26!
  • Everything starts with two letters: E (one dot, a short burst) and T (one dash, a long burst).
  • Dashed empty circles are for accented letters in other languages. Vietnamese does it differently: marks and special letters become extra letters (see the Việt tab).

Tips & Tricks to Decode Fast

1. Listen to the shape, don't count

Don't count "dot dot dash". Learn each letter as a little rhythm. Say dit (quick) and dah (long). Tap to hear:

2. Learn in 3 chunks, not 26 letters

Chunk 1: E T I A N M You can already spell MAN, TEAM, TIME, MINE.

Chunk 2: S U R W D K G O Now add GOD, WORD, SOUR, MARK.

Chunk 3: H V F L P J B X C Y Z Q Learn these as cousins of earlier letters using the tricks below.

3. The Opposites trick: swap dot ↔ dash

Flip every dot to a dash (and every dash to a dot) and you get another letter! Learn two letters for the price of one.

No opposite partner: C, H, V, Z. Just memorize those four.

4. The Mirror trick: read it backwards

Palindromes (same forwards and backwards)

5. Numbers are a pattern

1–5: that many dots, then dashes. 6–0: that many dashes, then dots.

5 = five dots. 0 = five dashes.

6. Practice with faith words

Tiếng Việt chips show the letters we send: the tone letter goes at the end of each syllable (Chúa = CHUAS).

7. Pro tips

  • Little and often: 5 minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
  • Spell your name and your saint's name again and again.
  • Don't stop on a mistake. Miss a letter? Let it go and catch the next one.
  • Send as well as receive. Try the Tap Key or flash a light.
  • Memorize SOS: three dots, three dashes, three dots. It can save a life.
